Rules this page applies (from the LST PDF)
  1. Asia range (LST 2.0): the high and low from 7:00 PM to 2:00 AM New York time are the liquidity levels. The range is labeled small (compressed energy, best days), normal or large (energy already spent in Asia) against the last 10 days.
  2. Liquidity grab: after Asia and until 12:00 PM NY, price trades above the Asia high (looking for a SELL, M pattern) or below the Asia low (looking for a BUY, W pattern). 🔥 PRIME marks confirmations in the prime hours: London 3:00–5:00 AM and New York 9:00–11:00 AM.
  3. Confirmation (Type 2, Manipulation Liquidity), always on a closed candle: Model 1: the grab candle closes back past the line, in the opposite half of the candle, with a visible rejection wick (damage). Model 2: the next candle is an engulfing that covers at least 50% of candle #1's body and closes back past the line. Model 3: the third candle closes back past the line with a solid body.
  4. Continuation (Type 1): if no model appears and price stays beyond the level, the page labels it "Type 1" and does not alert. If price returns inside without a model, a new sweep is evaluated again.
  5. Management: entry at the close of the model candle. Stop beyond the wick that took the liquidity, with some margin (never inside the model). At 50% of the move that took the liquidity: take 25–50% partials and move to break-even (never BE without partials). Target: the next liquidity, here the opposite side of the Asia range. At the target close 80% and leave 20% as the "pawn": stop at break-even, no take profit, trail the stop as price advances (pp. 179–180). Model 1 closing right at the line (variant 4) is flagged: it may only reach the 50%.
  6. Trend and risk:reward (pp. 103, 169, 172–175): the trend is read from price structure on the daily and H4 (higher highs + higher lows = up, lower highs + lower lows = down, otherwise ranging), with no indicators. Every signal is labeled ✅ with trend or ⚠️ against trend ("a model with the trend is worth 1,000 against it"). Against the trend, the target becomes the 50% (then 61.8%) of the last trend move, as the book says. Every signal shows its risk:reward; below 1:2 it is flagged, because the book says not to enter.
  7. Fibonacci pullback (LST 2.0 pp. 97, 138–144): once the direction is set (a confirmed model, or Type 1 continuation through the Asia level), the page measures the impulse from its origin to its extreme. In the pullback it waits for at least the 50% (best: the Golden zone 61.8–78.6%), for price to take an internal liquidity point (a swing low/high inside the impulse) or the first buyers/sellers of the correction, and for Model 1, 2 or 3 in favour of the trend. Stop beyond the sweep · 50% of the pullback for partials + BE · target: the impulse extreme. If the pullback reaches the origin, the setup is cancelled.

Setup B, the second chance (LST 2.0 pp. 182–186): after a confirmed model, the page finds the nearest S/R in the trade's direction (swing points before the model and the previous day's high/low). (A) Price must break it with force (a strong candle closing through it). (B) Price comes back to retest that zone with weaker, corrective candles. (C) A model confirms there, at the S/R line, an internal liquidity point or the first buyers/sellers of the retest. The page shows the risk:reward and flags anything below 1:2, where the book says to skip the trade.

Not automated yet (needs your eye): models at previous day/week highs and lows, Asia-session prime models (8–10 PM NY), the Asia range used as S/R after Type 1, and trendlines. This page only sends alerts. It never places trades.
