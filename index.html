Rules this page applies (from the LST PDF)

Symbols: 🔥 the model confirmed in the prime hours, 3:00–5:00 AM (London) or 9:00–11:00 AM (New York), NY time. ✅ the signal goes with the daily/H4 trend; ⚠️ it goes against it. ✓ 1:1 in Result: price moved at least as far as the risk before the trade ended. ✓ after a label on the chart: that trade has finished.

  1. Asia range (LST 2.0): the high and low from 7:00 PM to 2:00 AM New York time are the liquidity levels. The range is labeled small (compressed energy, best days), normal or large (energy already spent in Asia) against the last 10 days.
  2. Liquidity grab: after Asia and until 12:00 PM NY, price trades above the Asia high (looking for a SELL, M pattern) or below the Asia low (looking for a BUY, W pattern). 🔥 PRIME marks confirmations in the prime hours: London 3:00–5:00 AM and New York 9:00–11:00 AM.
  3. Confirmation (Type 2, Manipulation Liquidity), always on a closed candle: Model 1: the grab candle closes back past the line, in the opposite half of the candle, with a visible rejection wick (damage). Model 2: the next candle is an engulfing that covers at least 50% of candle #1's body and closes back past the line. Model 3: the third candle closes back past the line with a solid body.
  4. Continuation (Type 1): if no model appears and price stays beyond the level, the page labels it "Type 1" and does not alert. If price returns inside without a model, a new sweep is evaluated again.
  5. Management: entry at the close of the model candle. Stop by zones, never at the wick (pp. 170–171): for a SELL, beyond the next resistance (a previous swing high) above the model's wick; for a BUY, beyond the next support below it, plus a small margin. If there is no zone beyond the wick (e.g. a new high), the wick plus a margin is used and flagged. In a 10-year backtest this stop lost the least of all stop rules tested (the wick stop was hit more often). Only signals aiming for at least 2× the risk (R:R 1:2) are alerted (p.173: "if your win rate does not let you hold at least 1:2, simply do not enter"); the rest are shown greyed out. The 50% is still measured from the origin of the move to the wick. At 50% of the move that took the liquidity: take 25–50% partials and move to break-even (never BE without partials). Target: the next liquidity, here the opposite side of the Asia range. At the target close 80% and leave 20% as the "pawn": stop at break-even, no take profit, trail the stop as price advances (pp. 179–180). Model 1 closing right at the line (variant 4) is flagged: it may only reach the 50%. A Model 1 whose wick only grazes the line (less than 20% of the candle past it) is not accepted (p.109).
  6. Result of each signal: after the model closes, the page follows price: ❌ STOPPED (stop hit), ½ 50% hit · stop at BE (take partials, stop to entry), ➖ closed at BE (came back to entry after the 50%), 🎯 TARGET HIT. ✓ 1:1 means price moved at least as far as the risk before the trade ended. Signals flagged as too tight or below 1:2 still show what happened, but they are not alerted. If one candle touches both stop and target, it counts as stopped. Finished signals stop being highlighted. The stop used is the exact wick; your real stop has extra margin, as the book says.
  7. Trend and risk:reward (pp. 103, 169, 172–175): the trend is read from price structure on the daily and H4 (higher highs + higher lows = up, lower highs + lower lows = down, otherwise ranging), with no indicators. Every signal is labeled ✅ with trend or ⚠️ against trend ("a model with the trend is worth 1,000 against it"). Against the trend, the target becomes the 50% (then 61.8%) of the last trend move, as the book says. Every signal shows its risk:reward; below 1:2 it is flagged, because the book says not to enter.
  8. Fibonacci pullback (LST 2.0 pp. 97, 138–144): once the direction is set (a confirmed model, or Type 1 continuation through the Asia level), the page measures the impulse from its origin to its extreme. In the pullback it waits for at least the 50% (best: the Golden zone 61.8–78.6%), for price to take an internal liquidity point (a swing low/high inside the impulse) or the first buyers/sellers of the correction, and for Model 1, 2 or 3 in favour of the trend. Stop beyond the next S/R zone past the sweep · 50% of the pullback for partials + BE · target: the impulse extreme. If the pullback reaches the origin, the setup is cancelled.

Setup B, the second chance (LST 2.0 pp. 182–186): after a confirmed model, the page finds the nearest S/R in the trade's direction (swing points before the model and the previous day's high/low). (A) Price must break it with force (a strong candle closing through it). (B) Price comes back to retest that zone with weaker, corrective candles. (C) A model confirms there, at the S/R line, an internal liquidity point or the first buyers/sellers of the retest. The page shows the risk:reward and flags anything below 1:2, where the book says to skip the trade.

Backtest, 5 years (Nov 2021 – Nov 2026, Gold/EUR/USD/Bitcoin stand-ins, 15m–4h, stop by zones, half at the 50% + BE, rest at the target, no costs): about 3 of 4 signals reached the 50% (no loss). ⭐ Fibonacci pullbacks were the only setup positive every year (+0.02R to +0.15R per trade, +0.08R average); Asia and Setup B were close to break-even. Signals whose model closed already past the target (⛔ no target) lost 98% of the time, so the page marks them and does not alert them. Past results do not guarantee future results; this is not financial advice.

👁 WATCH (not tied to the Asia range): the page measures the last strong move (at least 5 average candles) and shows WATCH while price is back between the 50% and 78.6% of it, adding an S/R zone if earlier swing points sit there. WATCH is not a signal, only "look at this chart". It becomes W SELL / W BUY only when a Model 1, 2 or 3 closes in the pullback. Context only, never alerted, not in the history: this setup has not been backtested, and similar retests lost after costs. Zones drawn by hand can differ from the ones the page finds.

📈 Trend lines (LST 2.0 pp. 61–66, 103, 126): a trend line is a dynamic S/R joining at least two swing points (higher lows for a rising line, lower highs for a falling one) that no candle has closed through. The book says never to enter on the 2nd point: wait for price to move away with speed and come back with a corrective pullback, then look for Model 1, 2 or 3 at the next touch (touch #3 or later). A line with 3+ touches broken by a strong candle is watched for a retest from the other side. A touch counts within 30% of an average candle of the line. Shown as context only, never alerted: in the 5-year backtest the touch models were about break-even (+0.02R per trade before costs, −0.05R after) and the retests lost (−0.01R before costs, −0.09R after).

Real prices (important): the earlier backtests used the Binance stand-ins. Re-run on 5 years of real 1-minute prices (Gold, EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, GBP/JPY; spread + commission per trade: Gold $0.30, EUR/USD 1 pip, USD/JPY 1.2 pips, GBP/USD 1.4 pips, GBP/JPY 2.5 pips), with the page's stops and management: no setup has a reliable edge after costs. Fib: Gold −0.06R, EUR/USD −0.05R, USD/JPY +0.05R, GBP/USD +0.05R, GBP/JPY −0.01R per trade (about 300 trades each, too few to trust the small positives). Asia and Setup B were negative on all five. The page finds the book's models; it does not make them profitable. Past results do not guarantee future results; this is not financial advice.

Filters from the backtest (5 years, checked separately in 2021–23 and 2024–26 so they are not luck): (1) Weekends: Gold and EUR/USD are closed Friday 5 PM – Sunday 6 PM NY, but the Binance stand-ins keep trading, so models in that window are shown as CLOSED and never alerted. (2) ⛔ Stop too tight: when the spread + commission is more than 10% of the risk, the trade lost −0.12R to −0.36R on average, so it is flagged and not alerted. (3) ⭐ marks Fib pullback signals (not on Bitcoin, where costs are higher). With the stand-ins they looked positive, with real prices they did not (see above). (4) Tested and not added as alerts because they were break-even: London range high/low hunted in New York, yesterday's high/low (PDH/PDL), swing liquidity on S/R zones, trend lines.
